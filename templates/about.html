About ScamGuard Architecture

Detailed architectural breakdown of the multimodal recruitment fraud defense system.

1. Overview & Research Objectives

ScamGuard is an applied computer science research prototype engineered by a 5-member engineering team leading toward a provisional patent filing.

The platform addresses the rampant rise of predatory employment fraud targeting Indian college freshers and citizens across digital communication platforms (WhatsApp, Telegram, LinkedIn, SMS).

2. Multimodal Ingestion Pipeline

2.1 Conversational Messaging Analysis

Evaluates recruiter chat texts in five regional languages (English, Hindi, Hinglish, Bengali, Malayalam) using Gemini AI semantic reasoning to capture evasive phrasing and arbitrary financial demands.

2.2 Corporate Domain & SMTP Audit

Scrutinizes offer letter sender domains, detecting mismatches between advertised company websites and free public webmail addresses (e.g., @gmail.com, @yahoo.com).

2.3 Optical Character Recognition (OCR)

Extracts textual content from scanned PDFs and image-based appointment letters to uncover disguised registration fees, caution deposits, and omitted joining dates.

2.4 QR & UPI Forensic Inspector

Analyzes payment request screenshots to alert users when a scammer sends a collect-request disguised as a bonus disbursement.

3. Dual-Tier Hybrid Evaluation Engine

3.1 Hard Deterministic Safety Floor

A strict engineering invariant governs scoring: any rule score reaching 60 or above (indicating upfront fees, deposits, or credential harvesting) can never be lowered by the AI layer.

3.2 Prompt Injection Resistance

All user input is treated as untrusted data. Text instructing the scanner to "rate safe" or "ignore red flags" triggers an automatic high-risk violation.

4. Dynamic Database & National Helpline Coordination

4.1 Dynamic Risk Index Escalation

Offenders in the community blacklist are dynamically graded: < 5 reports (Low Risk), 5–9 reports (Medium Risk), and ≥ 10 reports (High Risk).

4.2 National Cybercrime Reporting Integration

Victims of active financial deception are routed directly to the Government Cybercrime Helpline (1930) and the official reporting portal (cybercrime.gov.in).
